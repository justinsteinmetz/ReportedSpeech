Section 1 of 6 · Foundations

What is reported speech?

Imagine you’re telling a friend about a conversation you had. You have two choices:

Direct: exact words
“I work in a bank,” said Daniel.
→
Reported: your words
Daniel said that he worked in a bank.

Two things happened when we reported Daniel’s words. The quotation marks disappeared, because we are no longer quoting. And the verb work shifted back to worked. This shift is called backshift.

Direct speechReported (indirect) speech
Quote the speaker’s exact wordsDescribe what was said
Words go in quotation marksUse a reporting verb + that / if / to
No change of tenseTense shifts one step back (backshift)

Why does this matter? Reported speech appears in news articles, academic writing, conversations and interviews. Every time you say “She told me that…” or “They asked whether…” you’re using it.

Four types to know

Statements
say + (that) + clause, or tell + person + (that) + clause. That is optional.
“I’m tired.” → She said that she was tired.
Yes / no questions
Use ask + if / whether + normal word order. Drop do / does / did.
“Are you coming?” → He asked if I was coming.
Wh- questions
Use ask + wh-word + normal subject–verb order. Drop do / does / did.
“Where do you live?” → She asked where I lived.
Commands & requests
Use tell / ask / advise + person + to-infinitive. Negative: not to.
“Open the window.” → She told me to open the window.

Say vs tell. Tell always needs a person straight after it. Say never does.

✓ She told me she was tired.   ✓ She said she was tired.   She said me she was tired.

Backshift: how tenses shift

When the reporting verb is past (said, told…), the verb inside the reported clause usually shifts one step back in time:

present simple“I work”
→
past simpleshe worked
past simple“I worked”
→
past perfectshe had worked
present perfect“I have worked”
→
past perfectshe had worked
will + infinitive“I will come”
→
would + infinitiveshe would come
can“I can swim”
→
couldshe could swim
going to“I’m going to leave”
→
was / were going toshe was going to leave

Examples in context

Direct speechIndirect speech
“Your times are really good.”She said my times were really good.
“I called you twice.”He said he had called me twice.
“I have never been to Paris.”She said she had never been to Paris.
“I’m sure you will qualify.”He said he was sure she would qualify.
“You can use the truck.”His dad said he could use the truck.

Time & place words shift too

They change when the reporting moment differs from the speaking moment. Report it five minutes later, in the same place, and today and here can stay exactly as they are.

now→then today→that day tonight→that night tomorrow→the next day yesterday→the day before last night→the night before last week→the week before next week→the following week ago→before here→there this→that these→those

When backshift changes, or doesn’t

Reported speech is about two moments: when the words were spoken, and when you report them. Backshift marks the distance between them.

Past reporting verb
Said, told, asked: the speaking moment is over, so tenses usually shift back.
“I’m tired.” → She said she was tired.
Present reporting verb
Says, tells, is asking: she is still speaking to you, so nothing shifts.
“I’m tired.” → She says she’s tired.
Still true or just said
Backshift is optional if the fact is still true, or you report the words straight away.
“The Earth orbits the Sun.” → She said the Earth orbits the Sun.
Some modals stay
would, could, should, might and ought to don’t change.
“You should rest.” → She said I should rest.

Past simple may stay. When the time is stated, or the order of events is obvious, English often keeps the past simple: “I called you at six.” → He said he called me at six. Had called is the safe, standard form.

Key rules to remember

1
Backshift tenses one step back when the reporting verb is in the past.
2
Use that for statements, if / whether for yes/no questions, and to-infinitive for commands.
3
Suggest is special: never with a to-infinitive. Use suggest + …ing or suggest that…
4
In indirect questions, use normal word order (subject–verb) and drop do / does / did.
5
Pronouns and time words change to fit the new speaker and the new moment: I → she, tonight → that night.
6
Would, could, should, might don’t shift. Backshift is optional when the fact is still true, and unnecessary after a present reporting verb (she says…).
